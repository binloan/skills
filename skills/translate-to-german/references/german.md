# German language file

Translate for the reader of this document. Read the source first: who writes to whom, why, and in what tone. From that, choose register, vocabulary, and sentence rhythm as a German writer in the same situation would. When the text leaves something open, decide, and say in the report what you assumed.

Decided for this project:
- de-DE, spelling with `ß`.
- Formal register: Sie. Informal: du, always lower case.
- Gendered terms: formal text uses the slash form with hyphen where it reads cleanly (`Mitarbeiter/-innen`). Use the slash form only on nouns. Keep articles and pronouns in a single form. Edge cases and informal text use the generic masculine.
- Instructions keep the form of the source: an imperative stays an imperative, an infinitive stays an infinitive.
- Keep currency symbols and codes, section numbering, and layout. Numbers take German separators.
- VAT: `MwSt.` in everyday text, `USt.` in business and tax text.
- Terms with legal or tax weight go into the report for review.

Before you finish: a consistent address form for each speaker–addressee relationship, natural German, numbers and dates in German form, and meaning unchanged.
